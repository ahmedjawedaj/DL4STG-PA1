\section{Task 2: Leaderboard Challenge}

\subsection{Data and what it implies}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Period recovery.} The spectrum and autocorrelation of the training target
        show a clear 24-step cycle (mean profile peaks at phase 0--1, trough at 14--16). The
        rest is broad low-frequency power with no single cycle dominating. Autocorrelation is
        0.97 at lag 1, 0.40 at lag 24, 0.16 at 48 and about 0.02 by lag 96, so most of the
        168-step horizon cannot be solved by short-lag persistence alone.
  \item \textbf{Heavy tail.} Median 73, 99th percentile 418, maximum 994. The level changes
        by an order of magnitude within days (Figure~\ref{fig:q2final}, left).
  \item \textbf{Optional file.} Six continuous variables (A, B, C look like smooth
        weather states, D, E, F are non-negative cumulative counters) and four one-hot
        indicators. The strongest contemporaneous links to the target are D ($-0.35$ with
        $\log(1+y)$), H ($-0.35$), A ($+0.31$) and I ($+0.23$). The file covers the hidden
        168 steps, so it can enter as a \emph{known-in-advance} input.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validation protocol}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Chronological three-way split into roughly year-long blocks (assuming the
        24-step cycle is one day, so a year is about 8\,760 steps): fit on $[0, 26304)$,
        early-stop on $[26304, 35064)$ (origins every 24 steps), compare designs on the
        final block, whose last 8\,568 steps $[35088, 43656)$ are tiled by
        \textbf{51 non-overlapping 168-step blocks}.
  \item Every block starts at phase 0, exactly like the hidden block (43\,656 is a
        multiple of 24), so per-step errors are comparable with the test week.
  \item Every configuration is trained with \textbf{3 seeds}. I report the seed mean and
        standard deviation, plus a paired per-block comparison (seed-averaged block
        RMSE, exact two-sided sign test over the 51 blocks). Seed standard deviation
        (0.6 to 4.2 RMSE, 10.4 for the log target) is as large as most design differences, so single-seed rankings were not used.
  \item I also tracked the 16 blocks nearest the start and end of the final block, because
        the hidden week has the same phase in the recovered 24-step cycle and may be closer
        to those edge blocks than to the middle of the year.
  \item The leaderboard was not used for any decision.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model}
Autoformer encoder-decoder written from scratch (\texttt{pa1q2/autoformer.py}),
following Wu et al.\ (2021) and the structure of the public reference
\texttt{thuml/Autoformer}, reusing the delay scoring and aggregation convention from
Question 1.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Series decomposition}: centred moving average (kernel 25, replicate
        padding) at the input and after every sublayer (2 per encoder layer, 3 per decoder
        layer). The decoder accumulates the three extracted trends through a projection
        into the trend path, which starts from the input trend (label part) and the window
        mean (horizon part).
  \item \textbf{Auto-Correlation}: FFT delay scores $R(\tau)$ from learned $q,k$,
        top-$k$ delays with $k=\lfloor \ln L\rfloor$ ($k=5$ in the encoder, 5 in the
        decoder), softmax weights and circular time-delay aggregation of $v$. It replaces
        dot-product attention in encoder self, decoder self and decoder cross mixing.
  \item Sizes: $L=168$ (one week), label length 48, \texttt{pred\_len} $=168$, one encoder
        and one decoder layer, $d_\text{model}=16$, 4 heads, $d_\text{ff}=32$, dropout
        0.1.
  \item \textbf{Inputs}: the target (globally standardised on the fit region, no
        log) as the value channel. Known-in-advance ``marks'' over the encoder window and
        the whole decoder window: index-derived phase features ($\sin$/$\cos$ with periods
        24 and 8\,766), the ten optional variables (D, E, F through $\log(1+x)$, all
        standardised on the fit region), their trailing 6- and 24-step means, and two
        origin-relative position features (steps since origin$/168$ and
        $e^{-h/24}$).
  \item Training: MSE on the standardised target, Adam, learning rate
        $3\cdot10^{-4}$ halved every epoch (reference schedule), batch 64, gradient clip 1,
        at most 6 epochs, early stopping with patience 2 on raw-scale RMSE. One epoch is a
        full pass over every stride-1 window of the fit region.
\end{itemize}

\bottomrule

\end{center}
Same architecture ($d_\text{model}=32$ base), 3 seeds each, holdout = 51 blocks of the
final year. Reference baselines on the same blocks: climatology 93.0, mean of the last
week 97.9, persistence 147.7, direct ridge without covariates 87.7, direct ridge with
known-horizon covariates 71.5 (about 311k weights).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Known-horizon covariates are the main source of skill}: RMSE falls from
        97.7 to 74.4 ($-24\%$), MAE from 72.2 to 51.7, winter RMSE from 119.4 to 88.2. Like for like
        ($d{=}32$), the covariate model beats the no-data model on 44 of 51 blocks (sign
        test $p\approx10^{-7}$). The selected small configuration beats it on 48 of 51.
  \item \textbf{Past-only covariates do nothing} (99.0 vs 97.7, within seed noise). At a
        168-step horizon, the conditions that drive the target \emph{during} the week
        matter, not the ones before the origin. This is the answer to ``which portions''
        and ``where they enter'': the horizon part must reach the decoder as a known input.
  \item \textbf{Without optional data the Autoformer is worse than climatology} (97.7 vs
        93.0). Its horizon trend starts at the window mean, which for this series is a
        noisy estimate of the next week's level (the last-week mean alone scores 97.9).
  \item Trailing 6/24-step covariate means help a little. Removing them raises mean block
        RMSE by 1.1 and is worse on 34 of 51 blocks, but the pooled RMSE is almost unchanged.
\end{itemize}

The two horizon-mark rows also use replicate instead of circular padding in the value
embedding and decoder trend projection, which removed an error spike at the last
horizon steps caused by wrap-around (step-168 RMSE 122 to 113 in the anchored pair).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Raw vs log target.} Training on $\log(1+y)$ gives the best MAE (49.4)
        and sMAPE (54.2) of any run but worse and unstable RMSE (81.6 $\pm$ 10.4). The
        back-transform under-predicts the heavy peaks, and RMSE punishes those misses more
        than MAE or sMAPE. Since ranking is by RMSE, I train on the raw scale.
  \item \textbf{Size.} Halving the width (23k to 7k or 11k parameters) costs nothing
        measurable. All covariate variants are within about 3 RMSE, inside seed spread,
        so the cheaper model is preferred under the $\alpha P+\beta E$ penalty.
  \item \textbf{Horizon position.} Autoformer has no positional encoding, so its decoder
        cannot tell step 1 from step 100. At step 1 the $d{=}16$ width-5 model's RMSE was 107 against
        25 for persistence. Adding origin-relative marks lowered ES RMSE (79.1 to 77.5)
        and holdout RMSE (74.5 to 73.2) at +320 parameters, and gave the best winter RMSE.
        Anchoring on the last value was not a clear single-seed win once the marks were
        present, but its seed-averaged holdout RMSE was slightly better. Starting the
        horizon trend at the last value failed badly in quick checks, probably because the
        smoothed decoder trend could not pull a spike back down.
  \item \textbf{Selection rule.} Among variants within noise, I mainly used full holdout
        RMSE, with winter RMSE and cost as tie-breakers. The two horizon-mark rows are very
        close (ES 77.5 vs 77.6, holdout 73.2 vs 72.9, paired block difference 0.15 with
        25 of 51 blocks each way). I kept the anchored row for the final forecast because
        its three-seed average had the best full-holdout RMSE, while preserving the
        non-anchored two-seed forecast as a fallback snapshot.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Final model and submission}
\begin{itemize}
  \item I refit the selected configuration on $[0, 35064)$ with the 51 holdout blocks as
        the early-stopping set. Based on the three-seed dev runs, I averaged seeds 0, 1
        and 2 after applying the same non-negativity floor. On the final-year early-stop
        blocks, the best checkpoints reached RMSE 68.3, 79.0 and 68.0 respectively. Seed
        1 was weak by itself, but it improved the same ensemble on the dev holdout.
  \item Forecast origin 43\,656 (0-based), i.e.\ \texttt{time\_idx} 43\,657 to 43\,824, in
        chronological order. Outputs below the 1st percentile of the fit-region target
        (7.0) are raised to it before averaging. On the dev holdout, the same three-seed
        anchored ensemble scored RMSE 69.34. I also saved the previous non-anchored
        seed-0/seed-2 forecast separately before replacing the active submission folder.
  \item \textbf{Declared $P = 33\,891$ trainable parameters and $E = 15$ epochs.} For an
        ensemble the leaderboard asks for the sum over the three runs:
        $11\,297 \times 3$ parameters and $6+3+6$ epochs. The commands below reproduce the
        three runs; the final prediction file is the average of their floored forecasts.
\begin{quote}\small
\begin{verbatim}
python -m pa1q2.train --cov future --seed 0 --d-model 16 --d-ff 32 \
  --mark-kernel 5 --horizon-mark 1 --anchor 1 --pad replicate \
  --stage final --out runs/final --tag final_anchor_rep
python -m pa1q2.train --cov future --seed 1 --d-model 16 --d-ff 32 \
  --mark-kernel 5 --horizon-mark 1 --anchor 1 --pad replicate \
  --stage final --out runs/final --tag final_anchor_rep
python -m pa1q2.train --cov future --seed 2 --d-model 16 --d-ff 32 \
  --mark-kernel 5 --horizon-mark 1 --anchor 1 --pad replicate \
  --stage final --out runs/final --tag final_anchor_rep
python -m pa1q2.submission runs/final/future-log0-ph1-L168-d16-s0-final_anchor_rep.json \
  runs/final/future-log0-ph1-L168-d16-s1-final_anchor_rep.json \
  runs/final/future-log0-ph1-L168-d16-s2-final_anchor_rep.json
\end{verbatim}
\end{quote}
  \item Sanity check only: the active forecast has 168 values in order from
        \texttt{time\_idx} 43\,657 to 43\,824, with minimum 7.0 after flooring.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Short reflections}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Q2.5 (one shot vs recursive).} The model is one-shot (direct). Errors do
        not compound, but early steps are less sharp than persistence because the model is
        optimizing all 168 steps together. Recursive rollout would make the first few steps
        easier but would accumulate errors over a heavy-tailed week.
  \item \textbf{Q2.6 (window normalisation).} Removing each window's level destroys the
        absolute level, which here carries information (very high levels tend to decay
        within days). Global scaling keeps it. Anchoring on the
        last value did not help once position marks were present.
  \item \textbf{Q2.7 (past vs known-horizon covariates).} The distinction changes where a
        covariate enters. Here the same ten variables were worthless as past-only encoder
        inputs and cut RMSE by 24\% when passed through the decoder marks.
  \item \textbf{Q2.8 (no timestamps).} Calendar effects (weekday, holidays, heating
        season start) are lost. The 24-step period was recovered from the spectrum and
        autocorrelation and encoded from the integer index. The yearly encoding (8\,766
        steps) is an assumption with weak support in the data. This is only
        trustworthy because the sampling is uniform with no gaps, as the task guarantees.
\end{itemize}
